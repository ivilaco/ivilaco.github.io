I am a PhD student in Economics at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA). My research lies at the intersection of development economics, applied microeconomics, history, and political economy. I combine applied econometric and machine learning methods with historical and qualitative evidence to study development in fragile settings, with particular interest in institutions, social norms, gender studies, conflict and organized crime, and exposure to violence in Latin America.

I hold an M.A. in Economics and a B.A. in Economics from Universidad del Rosario (Colombia), where I also completed a second master's degree in Public Policy Economics.

Prior to joining UCLA, I worked as a pre-doctoral research fellow with the Development Impact Group (DECDI/DIME) at the World Bank. I have also worked as a research fellow and consultant with academic institutions and international organizations, including Universidad del Rosario, Collegio Carlo Alberto, the United Nations, and the Bogotá Secretary of Education.

Outside academia, I enjoy playing and watching sports (especially tennis), traveling to experience new cultures, good food, and the art of great storytelling (through music, literature, and the screen).
